What happened at the house doesn't have to stay there.

Greek life runs on loyalty and silence. This is where the silence breaks. We collect anonymous accounts of sexual violence connected to fraternities and sororities on U.S. campuses. Every account is counted by campus and chapter, so the pattern is on the record even when the institutions look away.

Why chapters, and not names

Survivors are told their story is a single bad night, one bad guy, a misunderstanding. Chapter-level data tells a different story: the same houses, the same parties, the same group chats, year after year.

We ask where it happened and which chapter was involved. We do not publish the names of individuals, yours or anyone else's. That protects you, and it keeps the focus on the organizations and universities that let this continue.

Pledge of Silence is not a police report or a Title IX complaint. If you want either of those, our support page explains how, and nothing you share here stops you from doing it later.

The research

Decades of studies point in the same direction. These are the findings most often cited, with the original sources linked.

3×more likely to commit sexual assault

First-year men who joined fraternities were three times more likely to commit sexual assault than men who didn't join, even though the two groups looked similar when they arrived on campus.

Foubert, Newberry & Tatum (2007), NASPA Journal 44(4). Read the study

29%of sorority women reported being assaulted, vs. 7% of non-members

In a survey of 779 women at a public university, sorority members were four times as likely to have been sexually assaulted during college. The gap stayed large even after accounting for drinking and attendance at Greek parties.

Minow & Einolf (2009), Violence Against Women 15(7). Abstract on PubMed

26.4%of undergraduate women, at schools surveyed in 2015 and 2019

The largest campus climate survey in the U.S., with more than 181,000 students at 33 schools, found that nonconsensual sexual contact by force or incapacitation among undergraduate women went up between 2015 and 2019, not down.

Association of American Universities, 2019 Campus Climate Survey. AAU summary

87%of alcohol-involved campus assaults committed by repeat offenders

A decade of survey data from nearly 50 colleges found that most alcohol-involved sexual assaults were committed by men who did it more than once, and that fraternity members and athletes were more likely than other students to be among them.

Foubert, Clark-Taylor & Wall (2020), Violence Against Women. Summary of findings

4 in 5assaults of college women never reported to police

Federal crime data found that about 80% of rapes and sexual assaults of female college students went unreported to law enforcement. Accounts like the ones collected here are a large part of what official numbers miss.

Sinozich & Langton, U.S. Bureau of Justice Statistics (2014). BJS report (PDF)

27.5%of fraternity men surveyed had been assaulted or nearly assaulted

Men are harmed in Greek life too. In a random sample of fraternity members at a large Midwestern university, more than a quarter reported sexual assault or attempted assault since starting college.

Luetke et al. (2020). Abstract on PubMed

Research on this topic uses different definitions and survey methods, so exact figures vary between studies. The direction of the findings has been consistent for more than thirty years.

Cases

A small sample of cases that reached the news. For every one of these there are countless accounts that never did. Where cases are unresolved, allegations are described as allegations.

  1. 2010 to 2011

    Yale University, Delta Kappa Epsilon

    Chapter banned from campus for five years

    Pledges were filmed chanting slogans endorsing rape outside Yale's Women's Center. Students later filed a federal Title IX complaint describing a hostile environment at Yale, and the university suspended the chapter.

  2. 2015 to 2016

    Stanford University, Kappa Alpha party

    Conviction; six-month jail sentence

    A woman who had attended a Kappa Alpha party was assaulted outside while unconscious. Her attacker was convicted of three felonies and served three months. Public anger over the sentence led voters to recall the judge in 2018. The survivor, Chanel Miller, later told her story in the memoir Know My Name.

    Case background

  3. 2021

    University of Southern California, Sigma Nu

    Chapter suspended; police investigation

    USC issued a campus crime alert after reports of a sexual assault at the Sigma Nu house and of drugs slipped into drinks at a party there. The chapter was suspended, the chapter president was suspended by the university, and students protested outside the house. More reports involving other houses followed within days.

    ABC7 Los Angeles

  4. 2024 to present

    Cornell University, Chi Phi

    Civil lawsuit filed September 2026; criminal case reopened

    A former student alleges she was drugged and sexually assaulted for hours by seven fraternity members after one of them invited others through the chapter's group chat. Cornell barred the chapter, but her lawsuit says only two of the seven were expelled. No criminal charges were filed at first. After the lawsuit drew national attention, the Tompkins County District Attorney announced he was reopening the investigation.

    CBS New York and ABC News

Medical care

Forensic medical exam (rape kit)

Available at many hospital emergency rooms, ideally within 5 days (120 hours). It treats injuries and preserves evidence in case you decide to report later. Under federal law, you can have the exam without reporting to police and without paying out of pocket for the exam itself.

Ask for a SANE (sexual assault nurse examiner). RAINN can tell you which local hospital has one: 800.656.4673.

HIV prevention (PEP)

Post-exposure prophylaxis can prevent HIV if started within 72 hours, and works best the sooner you start. Ask for it at the ER, an urgent care, or a health center.

Emergency contraception

Levonorgestrel pills (Plan B and generics) are sold over the counter without a prescription or ID. They work best within 72 hours. Health centers and Planned Parenthood can advise on other options that work up to 5 days.

STI testing and treatment

Available at campus health centers, community health centers, and Planned Parenthood, often free or on a sliding scale.

If you don't have insurance

The forensic exam

Federal law requires states to make sure survivors aren't billed out of pocket for the forensic exam. If you get a bill for the exam itself, call the hospital billing office and your local rape crisis center; advocates deal with this regularly. Other ER charges may still be billed, and victim compensation (below) can often cover them.

Crime victim compensation

Every state has a fund that can pay medical bills, counseling, and lost wages for victims of violent crime, including sexual assault. Rules differ by state and many require some kind of report to police or a medical provider, so ask an advocate before you apply.

Find your state's program (NACVCB)

Community health centers

Federally funded clinics charge on a sliding scale based on income, and many see patients who can't pay at all. They provide primary care, STI testing, and often mental health care.

Find a health center near you (HRSA)

Planned Parenthood

STI testing, emergency contraception, and pregnancy care, with sliding-scale fees at many locations.

Find a health center

Your campus

If you're a current student, campus health and counseling services are usually free or already covered by your fees, whether or not you file a report.

Legal and campus rights

Title IX

Schools that receive federal funding must respond to sexual harassment and assault, including by offering supportive measures like class, housing, or schedule changes, even if you don't file a formal complaint. If your school mishandles your case, you can file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Education's Office for Civil Rights, generally within 180 days.

Know Your IX explains your rights in plain language. File with OCR.

Victim Rights Law Center

Free legal help for sexual assault survivors, including students navigating school proceedings, privacy, housing, and employment.

victimrights.org

End Rape On Campus

Survivor-founded organization offering direct support and help filing complaints against schools.

endrapeoncampus.org

National Crime Victim Law Institute

Can help connect survivors with attorneys who protect their rights in criminal cases.

ncvli.org

If it happened years ago

There is no deadline on getting support. Rape crisis centers and hotlines work with survivors whose assaults happened decades ago, and it's common for memories and feelings to resurface when a case like Cornell's is in the news.

Legal deadlines vary by state and by the type of case. Some states have lengthened or removed time limits for sexual assault claims, and a few have opened temporary windows for older civil claims. A free consultation with a survivor-focused attorney or the Victim Rights Law Center can tell you what's possible where you live.

Privacy and how this works

What we collect

Only what you type into the form. We don't ask for your name, email, or phone number. The form runs on CryptPad, an open-source service that encrypts your answers before they leave your device, so CryptPad itself can't read them. Only Pledge of Silence can.

What we publish

Counts of accounts by campus, state, chapter, and decade. If you give permission, we also publish your account after removing names and anything else that could identify you or another person. Every account is read by a person before anything is published.

What we don't do

We don't publish the names of individuals, share accounts with schools, police, or fraternity organizations, or contact you. Because we don't know who you are, we can't follow up, so if you want help, please use the support page.

If you change your mind

Because accounts are anonymous, we can't look yours up. If you want an account removed, write to us describing the campus, chapter, and year, and we'll remove matching accounts where we can.

An email address can identify you. To stay anonymous, write from a new account that isn't linked to your name or phone number, such as a free Gmail or Proton Mail account set up under a made-up name.

Staying safe

The Leave now button at the top of every page, or pressing Esc twice, takes you straight to a weather page. It doesn't erase your browser history, so if someone else uses your device, a private window is the safest choice.
